### verdict_on_novelty
DENTED, not dead — and it must be reworded. (1) The strong form 'prior XBRL/financial-numeric-extraction work reports accuracy but NOT cost or latency' is intact for the XBRL-tagging line (FiNER-139, FNXL, FinTagging, FLAN-FinXC, XBRLTagRec, HiFi-KPI, FinAuditing, FinBen, PIXIU, FLaME, BizFinBench: none report per-model cost or latency; arXiv XBRL AND latency/cost query = 0 hits), but it is contradicted by FinLoRA (arXiv 2505.19819), which fine-tunes Llama 3.1 8B on FiNER-139 and FNXL and plots inference time vs a Gemini API model (no dollar inference cost), and by arXiv 2603.22651, which reports cost per document, p50 latency and an explicit cost-accuracy Pareto frontier for 25-field extraction from 10,000 SEC 10-K/10-Q/8-K filings using GPT-4o/Claude/Gemini/Llama-70B/Mixtral. (2) The narrower form 'nobody has benchmarked small (1-3B) or distilled models on FinTagging with cost or latency' is intact: FinTagging has only 4 citing papers, none fine-tune small models, and small models are only evaluated zero-shot (Qwen3-0.6B FinNI F1 0.0038, Llama-3.2-3B 0.146). For FiNER/FNXL it holds only below 8B and only for dollar cost / quantized serving, since FinLoRA already covers 8B latency. (3) The format itself — small tuned model vs frontier API on accuracy, cost and latency — is well-trodden by 2025-2026: clinical NER (2501.00031: '101x cheaper and 12x faster'), legal contract extraction (2605.05532: $0.018 vs $0.149-$0.456/doc), general RE with 360M-3B models and ms-level latency (2606.22606), finance merchant extraction with a 270M-8B F1-vs-latency Pareto (2606.08051), and general cost-aware evaluation frameworks (Cost-of-Pass 2504.13359; AI Agents That Matter 2407.01502). Defensible claim: 'first accuracy/cost/latency frontier for sub-3B fine-tuned and quantized models, served with vLLM, on a public XBRL benchmark (FinTagging), against frontier APIs' — and it should cite FinLoRA, 2603.22651 and 2606.08051 as the closest analogs rather than claiming the gap is unaddressed.
