### title
FiNER: Financial Numeric Entity Recognition for XBRL Tagging

### authors

### task_summary
Introduces XBRL tagging as a token-level entity-extraction task: given a sentence from the text notes of a 10-K/10-Q, label (mostly numeric) tokens with one of 139 US-GAAP XBRL concept tags (IOB2 => 279 token labels). TEXT ONLY: sentences are regex-extracted from the Financial Statements Item notes, html-stripped and lower-cased; tables are NOT part of the dataset. Output is a per-token concept tag only — no value normalisation, no period, no unit. Key finding: BERT subword fragmentation of numbers hurts; replacing numbers with [NUM] or [SHAPE] pseudo-tokens fixes it; SEC-BERT-SHAPE (pre-trained with the pseudo-tokens) is best. A 'business use case' section evaluates top-k tag recommendation (Hits@10 = 99.4%).

### models_and_results
All models are BERT-base scale or smaller (Table 7 params: BiLSTM 0.8M-21M; every BERT variant 110M). Test entity-level micro-F1 (avg of 3 seeds): spaCy 48.6; BiLSTM(words) 77.3; BiLSTM(subwords) 71.3; BERT 75.1; BERT+CRF 78.0; BERT+[NUM] 78.3; BERT+[SHAPE] 79.4; FIN-BERT 74.0; FIN-BERT+[NUM] 78.8; FIN-BERT+[SHAPE] 80.1; SEC-BERT 75.7; SEC-BERT-NUM 80.4; SEC-BERT-SHAPE 82.1 (macro-F1 80.1; dev micro-F1 85.3). SEC-BERT was pre-trained from scratch on EDGAR-CORPUS (model card: '260,773 10-K filings from 1993-2019'; 'a single Google Cloud TPU v3-8'; '1 million training steps with batches of 256 sequences of length 512') [VERIFIED]. Derived community checkpoints on HF (none report latency): shidey/secbert-finer139 (test F1 0.8181, P 0.8018, R 0.8352); nbroad/finer-139-xtremedistil-l12-h384 (33.3M params, F1 0.6334, MIT); muhtasham/bert-tiny-finetuned-finer-139-full-intel-cpu (4.41M params, no model card/metrics); baluyotraf/distilbert-base-uncased-on-mini-finer (4 labels only, F1 0.969).

### small_models_evaluated
In the paper: only encoder/RNN models — BiLSTM (0.8M-21M params), BERT-base, FIN-BERT, SEC-BERT (all 110M). No decoder LLMs, no 1-3B models. Follow-ups on FiNER-139: FinLoRA (arXiv 2505.19819) uses Llama 3.1 8B (+LoRA / QLoRA 4-bit) and 70B on a 10.0k-train / 7.4k-test subset — nothing at 1-3B. FLAN-FinXC (2405.06671, NAACL 2024) uses FLAN-T5-Base 220M / Large 780M with LoRA. FinTagging (2505.20650) evaluates Qwen2.5-Instruct 0.5B-14B and LLaMA-3.2-3B zero-shot plus fine-tuned BERT-large/FinBERT/SECBERT, but on its own benchmark, not FiNER-139. Community HF checkpoints exist at 4.4M (bert-tiny) and 33M (XtremeDistil) but report no latency.

### relevance_to_idea
For FiNER-139 itself the novelty claim HOLDS: the paper reports accuracy only (micro/macro-F1, Hits@k) with no latency, throughput, serving or training cost. BUT the strong form ('nobody has benchmarked small or distilled models on FiNER-139/FNXL/FiNER with cost or latency') is PARTLY FALSIFIED by follow-ups the project must cite: (1) FinLoRA (arXiv 2505.19819, May 2025) fine-tunes Llama-3.1-8B with LoRA/QLoRA(4-bit) on a FiNER-139 subset (10.0k train / 7.4k test) and reports accuracy/F1 (base 8B 21.28/0.232; 70B 61.82/0.606; DeepSeek-V3 68.92/0.699; GPT-4o 72.29/0.725; LoRA-8bit-r8 74.10/0.759; QLoRA-4bit-r4 74.32/0.760), a fine-tuning $ cost ('Using four NVIDIA A5000 GPUs ... approximately 56.4 to 63.6 GPU hours ... At an estimated rate of $0.26 per GPU hour, this translates to a cost of roughly $14.66 to $16.54') and an inference-time figure ('Figure 3 illustrates the inference time of fine-tuned models on various datasets. Gemini API generally exhibits lower inference latency ... than local Llama 3.1 8B Instruct inference'). So cost + latency on FiNER-139 with an 8B model already exists — though not at 1-3B, not vLLM/quantized serving throughput, not $ per 1k facts, and only on a 7.4k subset. (2) FinTagging (2505.20650, v5 May 2026) already evaluates Qwen2.5-Instruct 0.5B-14B and LLaMA-3.2-3B zero-shot plus fine-tuned BERT-large/FinBERT/SECBERT, with no cost/latency sentences at all. (3) FLAN-FinXC (2405.06671) reports V100 '56 minutes/epoch' and '2 minutes/sample' inference for 220M-780M FLAN-T5 but no $. (4) A Mar-2026 multi-agent SEC-filing benchmark (2603.22651) reports $/doc ($0.148-$0.430) and p50/p95 latency, but on a private 10k-filing corpus with frontier/70B models only. Recommended narrowed claim: 'no fine-tuned 1-3B (or quantized) model has been evaluated on FinTagging (full 17,388-concept taxonomy, text + tables) with served cost per fact and p50/p95 latency / throughput against frontier APIs on the same test set.' Reuse: the [SHAPE]/[NUM] number-pseudo-token trick and SEC-BERT-SHAPE (82.1 micro-F1) as an encoder baseline; FiNER-139 train split as cheap auxiliary/distillation data (CC-BY-SA-4.0 — share-alike implications for released derivatives; the Zenodo copy says CC-BY-4.0). Avoid: describing FiNER-139 as containing tables (note-text sentences only), as covering value/period/unit (it tags concept names only), or comparing LLM numbers from the 7.4k FinLoRA subset with the 108k full test set.
