# Methods

## Study design

We evaluated five first-pass checkpoints and the final adapter from one Qwen3-1.7B fine-tuning run on the same held-out development split of SEC filings. The task was numeric identification (FinNI), following [Wang et al., *FinTagging: Benchmarking LLMs for Extracting and Structuring Financial Information*, arXiv:2505.20650v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20650v5): given a text block or table, return numeric values and their datatypes. We did not score concept linking, period, unit, scale, or sign as separate fields. The public FinNI-eval-v2 release (revision `e3e0aba23b5c3df17a71c8c013030182d013a538`) supplied the prompt, the reference distribution for sampling negative contexts, and the reference contexts for overlap screening. Its rows were not training examples or the test observations underlying the [Results](./results.md).

## Filings, labels, and split construction

The [filing selector](../../scripts/select_train_accessions.py) drew from SEC monthly XBRL RSS entries for 10-K and 10-Q filings with an inline XBRL primary document and filing dates from 2021-02-13 through 2023-02-12, before the benchmark filing window. Selection used seed `20261002`, equal form quotas, and SEC-office and filer-size strata derived from the recovered benchmark manifest. It excluded CIKs in the [recovered manifest](../../data/eval_manifest.csv), the [supplementary EDGAR submissions list](../research/edgar_submissions_train10.json), and the [unconfirmed candidate list](../../data/eval_cik_candidates_unconfirmed.txt). Candidates that could not be serialized because of an unknown US-GAAP concept or yielded no positive context were rejected. The [frozen accession list](../../data/train_accessions.txt) contains 338 filings, 169 of each form, actually filed between 2021-02-16 and 2023-02-10. It contains one filing per SEC Central Index Key (CIK); the held-out assignment therefore separated both filings and companies before any context sampling (321 training filings and 17 development filings).

The [serializer](../../ixbrl_facts/fintagging.py) rebuilt narrative blocks outside table cells and HTML tables from the filings. Labels came from inline `ix:nonFraction` tags with `us-gaap:` concepts and nonempty displayed values, with commas removed and duplicates collapsed by value and concept within the context. Concept types came from the pinned US-GAAP schemas in the [configuration](../config/thresholds.yaml); the retained types were integer, monetary, per-share, percent, and shares. A context was positive if it had at least one retained label. The [corpus serialization step](../../scripts/serialize_corpus.py) dropped empty-label contexts whose text matched a positive in the same filing. Remaining negatives were eligible only if narrative text had strictly between 100 and 500 characters or serialized table text contained at least 50 digit characters.

After overlap exclusions, the [corpus builder](../../scripts/build_corpus.py) kept the available positives and sampled negatives separately within each split, targeting 40% negatives and using the table share among deduplicated FinNI negatives to set the table quota, subject to availability. It used split-specific random streams derived from seed `20261003` to sample negatives and shuffle each split once. The [corpus report](../../data/corpus_report.json) records 38,788 training contexts (23,273 positive and 15,515 negative) and 1,933 development contexts (1,160 positive and 773 negative), below the respective caps of 40,000 and 2,000. The corpus hashes and accession assignments identify the exact splits used.

## Overlap screening and label limitations

The [overlap audit](../../scripts/contamination_audit.py) compared eligible training and development contexts against 25,765 distinct contexts from the pinned benchmark release. It stripped HTML tags, decoded entities, lowercased the text, removed ASCII punctuation, and split on whitespace. A context was flagged for a shared 13-word gram (the whole nonempty token sequence for shorter contexts), or for estimated Jaccard similarity of at least 0.80 on word 5-gram sets using 128 MinHash permutations. The MinHash path first retrieved candidates by locality-sensitive hashing (nine bands of 13 signature entries), then estimated similarity by the fraction of matching entries in the full signatures.

The [pool audit](../../data/contamination_audit/summary_pool.json) flagged 18,184 of 215,284 eligible contexts: 12,867 by the exact-gram rule alone and 5,317 by both rules, with none flagged by MinHash alone. All flagged IDs were excluded before sampling. Applying the same checks to the resulting 40,721 training and development contexts yielded [zero flags](../../data/contamination_audit/summary_final.json). This is a result for the implemented screen, not proof of no overlap: candidate retrieval can miss near duplicates, and neither rule rules out paraphrase or shared underlying facts. The filing-level exclusion was also incomplete: [manifest recovery](../OPEN.md) identified 139 filings across 129 of the 142 benchmark registrants. The content screen used the released benchmark contexts regardless of whether their source filings could be recovered. Neither check rules out prior exposure of the base model to public filings or their inline tags. The screen compared the internal corpus to the public benchmark, not training contexts to development contexts.

Training and development labels were automatic. The [planned two-pass human audit](../OPEN.md) was not completed, so we report neither a manual label-error estimate nor agreement. The planned public benchmark and external-model comparisons are outside this dev-only study.

## Fine-tuning and checkpoints

We fine-tuned `Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B` at revision `70d244cc86ccca08cf5af4e1e306ecf908b1ad5e` with seed 1 on Kaggle NVIDIA T4 hardware. The [training implementation](../../training/kaggle_train.py) used Hugging Face Trainer with PEFT, a bitsandbytes 4-bit NF4 base with fp16 computation, and gradient checkpointing. LoRA used rank 16, alpha 32, and zero dropout on the query, key, value, output, gate, up, and down projections. The microbatch was one instance with 16 gradient accumulation steps, and the learning rate was constant at 0.0001 with no warmup. The [shared prompt formatter](../../xbrl_frontier/sft_format.py) inserted each context into the FinNI query and rendered a single user turn with Qwen's chat template, the assistant generation prefix, and thinking disabled. The completion used the `{"result":[{"Fact": ..., "Type": ...}]}` schema, followed by the template's turn ending. Only completion tokens contributed to the loss; prompt and padding tokens were masked.

The trainer skipped rather than truncated 1,067 prompt-plus-completion sequences longer than 4,096 tokens, leaving 37,721 distinct instances. It traversed the filtered corpus in its saved order each epoch. The completed run manifest records first-pass snapshots at 2,512, 5,008, 10,000, 20,000 and 37,721 distinct instances, and a final adapter at step 4,716 after two epochs of 2,358 optimizer steps each. The first two snapshots crossed their nominal prefix targets at batch boundaries. The [learning-curve table](./learning-curve.md) records actual steps, source paths and hashes. These are snapshots of a single trajectory, not separate models trained from scratch at each sample size. No independent training-size validation run or additional training seed contributed to this comparison.

## Decoding, scoring, and uncertainty

All checkpoints used the same saved prompt template and [runner](../../serving/generate_predictions.py). Their local decode manifests record vLLM 0.30.0, fp16 inference with the PEFT adapter loaded on the pinned base, and a 32,768-token context limit accepted without fallback. Decoding was greedy (temperature 0) on one visible T4 per run. The [input-only output cap](../../xbrl_frontier/output_cap.py) was 64 plus 24 tokens per numeric-like span (digit expressions, standalone dashes, and listed number or nil words), bounded by the remaining context window. Prompts were not truncated, and each checkpoint produced one response for each of the 1,933 development IDs. The [table](./learning-curve.md) identifies the local-only artifacts and their hashes.

The [scoring entry point](../../scripts/score_predictions.py) joined responses to automatic labels by ID and scored cut-off responses as written. The [parser and scorer](../../xbrl_frontier/scorer.py) stripped thinking blocks, accepted fenced JSON or extracted a JSON object from surrounding text, and ignored malformed items. An unparseable response counted as an empty prediction, without regeneration. Within each context, scoring used exact set matches on `(normalized value, stripped type)` pairs. Normalization removed dollar signs, standalone `USD`, percent signs, and commas, collapsed whitespace, and lowercased the value string without expanding spelled-out magnitudes. We pooled true positives, false positives, and false negatives over contexts before computing micro-precision, recall, and F1. Raw scoring retained all 1,933 contexts; the primary deduplicated analysis retained the first occurrence of each exact context–answer pair in development-set order, leaving 1,920 contexts. Raw, deduplicated and context-type F1 are in the [six-row table](./learning-curve.md); per-instance counts remain in the local score reports.

For the primary contrast (final minus 10,000-instance deduplicated micro-F1), we mapped accession prefixes in the saved instance IDs to the 17 development CIKs. In each of 1,000 [paired bootstrap](../../xbrl_frontier/bootstrap.py) replicates (seed `20261010`), we drew 17 CIKs with replacement and used the same draw for both checkpoints. We pooled counts within each arm, recomputed micro-F1, and took the difference. We report the full-split difference with a two-sided 95% percentile interval from the resampled differences. The interval captures company-level sampling variation conditional on the fitted checkpoints; it does not estimate between-seed variability.
